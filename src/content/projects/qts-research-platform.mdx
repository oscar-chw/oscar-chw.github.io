## The problem

The research team needed tick-level Chinese A-share and crypto data that they could trust, query in seconds, and reproduce later on exactly the same rows. The data arrived as compressed vendor archives, each with its own quirks, and no one on the team was building the infrastructure full time. As the only developer, I owned the whole stack.

## What I built

- **A layered store.** Raw deliveries are kept byte-identical. A typed, <Term t="versioned Parquet">versioned Parquet layer</Term> sits above them, and a cleansed layer above that flags bad rows instead of dropping them.
- **A query service for researchers** in Python over HTTP and SQL. It returns Apache Arrow, pins every read to a data version, and <Term t="look-ahead bias">refuses requests that would see the future</Term>.
- **Data cleaning, led with the team:** exact duplicates removed, look-ahead leaks blocked before they reach research data.
- **The shared GPU research server**, which I administer: per-member data permissions through custom Linux PAM and eBPF modules in C, single sign-on without a VPN, and live health monitoring with transition-based alerting.
- **A market-data relay in Go** that terminates TLS and scrubs addresses for HTTP and WebSocket traffic, carrying ~300 Mbit/s of live market data over ~850 concurrent connections for every data collector on the server. It moved that data off the VPN and replaced a Python prototype at ~60% less CPU. Its 24-hour acceptance check passed all 14 checks: remote-login drops fell from 23 in 3 hours before the relay to 0 in 24 hours. It was tested with a property test (20,000 random inputs), a 22-case black-box suite, 13 caught mutants and two independent reviews.
- **Per-person AI coding agents on the research server**, behind a destructive-command guard: deletions go to a bin with an approval step, and the guard blocks all 33 bypasses an independent review found.

## Result

- 700B+ rows stored across A-share ticks (2017–2026), crypto market data and Polymarket order books.
- A full stock-day loads in under 0.2 s.
- A race test with 12 readers and 5 writers (4 appending, 1 rewriting a partition) kept all 23 of 23 commits.
- 500M+ duplicate rows removed.

Three decisions shaped it:

- **Flag, never drop.** Only exact duplicates are deleted. A cleansing rule that silently removes rows is a rule nobody can audit.
- **Three layers, not two.** When a cleansing rule changes, it reruns from the typed layer instead of re-decoding archives, and every rule's effect stays visible.
- **Safety over peak speed.** The query interface is deliberately slower than hand-written DuckDB SQL. In exchange it gives pinned versions, explicit errors and no look-ahead. Raw DuckDB is still there for those who want it.
