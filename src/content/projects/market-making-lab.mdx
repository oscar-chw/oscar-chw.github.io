## The problem

A market-making strategy that looks profitable in a naive simulator often is not. The simulator fills every quote, ignores where the order sits in the queue, and values inventory at prices that came later. After [IMC Prosperity 4](../imc-prosperity-4/) I wanted tooling that reviews a quoting policy the way a risk desk would. It contains none of the team's competition code.

## What I built

- **<Term t="market making">An inventory-aware quoting policy</Term>**: the reservation price shifts against the position the book is carrying.
- **A fill simulator** with a declared queue position, partial fills and cancel delays, run across 18 synthetic scenarios.
- **A causal analyzer** for saved runs that reports cash, fees, inventory-limit breaches and fill markouts.

## Result

Across 18 synthetic scenarios, <Term t="markouts">fill quality is measured with as-of markouts</Term>: each fill is valued only against prices already observed when it happened, and the forward markout is computed separately afterwards.

- **Money is exact decimals**, never binary floats.
- **Missing fees or identities are never guessed.** Ambiguous logs are refused.
- **Every run records hashes** of its inputs and of the installed code, and never overwrites an earlier output.

## Limits

- Scenarios are synthetic, and only one pinned community log format is supported.
- Inputs are capped in size, and sampled reports do not preserve extremes.
