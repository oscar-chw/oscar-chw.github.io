### The problem

A trading bot is mostly plumbing: fetching prices, turning them into a decision, protecting the position, sending orders and confirming fills, and keeping a record that adds up. If a fill is assumed instead of confirmed, or the backtest runs different code from the live loop, nothing the bot reports can be trusted.

### What I built

After the WQT 2025 hackathon I rebuilt my competition bot into a general, exchange-agnostic pipeline, adapted to the crypto desk blueprint:

- **Market data** from Binance through ccxt, with a fallback source.
- **Signals:** MACD, RSI, Bollinger bands, volume, engulfing candles and order-book imbalance, combined into one entry decision. The strategy is a simple, swappable example; the point is the pipeline around it.
- **Risk limits** before every order: a −2% stop, a tiered exit ladder up to +5%, a per-coin daily loss cap, a 72-hour holding limit and a 10% drawdown guard.
- **Market orders through a broker you choose:** paper trading by default, or any exchange ccxt supports. A position opens or closes only on a confirmed fill.
- **Records:** every order and close in JSONL with a CSV mirror, and a daily P&L file that counts each close once.
- **A backtester that runs the same decision code** as the live loop.

### Result

- The live loop and the backtest make the same entry decision on all 130 checked bars.
- 56 tests in three tiers, and 58 blueprint conformance tests for its data, feature, strategy and execution stages. Its risk and sizing stages do not pass the blueprint's tests yet.

### Limits

- The strategy is a simple example. This page makes no claim about its returns.
- Orders are market orders, so every trade pays the spread.
- Live and backtest agreement was checked on 130 bars, not on every bar the bot saw.
