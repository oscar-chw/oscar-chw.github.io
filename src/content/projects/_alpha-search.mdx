### The problem

Generating formulaic alphas is cheap. Evaluating them honestly is the hard part, because every extra look at the data quietly overfits. A search that breeds thousands of formulas will always find something that looks good in-sample; the question is whether the process can tell a real signal from luck.

### What I built

A <Term t="genetic programming">genetic-programming search</Term> over formulaic alpha expressions, run on 6.7 years of public Binance daily data for 34 coins:

- **Pre-registered splits.** Formulas may breed only on train data, are chosen on validation, and get exactly one test score.
- **<Term t="delay-1">Delay-1 signals with trading costs</Term>.** A signal computed today can trade only tomorrow, after costs.
- **An equal-budget random-search control**, sharing the GP's filter and validation rule, so any difference comes from breeding alone.
- **A grammar parser instead of eval.** Every candidate passes through the same AST parser, so a hostile line cannot run.

### Limits

- The coin universe is survivorship-biased: only coins that exist today.
- The cost and portfolio model is simple, with no borrow or funding.
- The repository reports its own result in full. This page states the method only.
